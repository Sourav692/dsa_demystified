42 · Trapping Rain Water

Rain falls on a row of bars. How much water stays behind?

The problem, in plain words

Each number is the height of a bar, one unit wide. After rain, water sits in the dips between bars. Over any one bar, the water rises to the lower of the tallest wall on its left and the tallest wall on its right. Add up the water over every bar.

Example: [0,1,0,2,1,0,1,3,2,1,2,1] → 6 units of water.
One bar's rule: water = min(tallest on the left, tallest on the right) - its own height. Both "tallest" values include the bar itself, so this is never negative.
looking attallest wall usedwaterfinished bar

The small number under each bar is its index.

Solution 1 — Brute force: for every bar, scan both sides

Stand on bar i. Look all the way left for the tallest wall, then all the way right for the tallest wall. The lower of the two is the water line above you. Do it again, from scratch, for the next bar.

Speed & memory: each bar rescans the whole row — O(n²) time, O(1) memory. The notebook measured about 3.3–4.9× per doubling.

Solution 2 — Better: write the tallest walls down once

The brute force keeps re-finding the same tallest walls. Instead, sweep once left to right and write down "tallest so far" for every bar (left), then once right to left (right). Now each bar's water is a quick lookup: min(left[i], right[i]) - height[i].
left[i] — tallest in height[0..i]
right[i] — tallest in height[i..n-1]

Speed & memory: three straight passes — O(n) time, but two extra lists — O(n) memory.

Solution 3 — The smart way: two fingers, move the lower side

One finger at each end, each remembering the tallest wall it has passed (left_max, right_max). If left_max < right_max, the next bar on the left has a wall at least right_max tall somewhere to its right, so its water line is just left_max. Step the left finger in, fill that bar, repeat. Otherwise do the same on the right.

Speed & memory: every step fills one bar and moves one finger — O(n) time, O(1) memory. The two lists of Solution 2 shrink to two numbers.

When you'd use this

See "how much water is trapped", an elevation map, or any per-position answer limited by the lower of the tallest thing on each side? Write down left and right maxima, then squeeze them into two fingers that always move the lower side.
