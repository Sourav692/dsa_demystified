295 · Find Median from Data Stream

Numbers arrive one at a time. After each one, what is the middle value?

The problem, in plain words

Build a small object with two buttons: addNum(x) drops a number in, and findMedian() tells you the middle value of everything dropped in so far.

The median is the middle value once the numbers are lined up in order. With an even count there are two middle values, so take their average. Think of a live dashboard showing the "typical" response time while new times keep arriving.
Example stream 5, 15, 1, 3, 8 → medians after each number: 5.0, 10.0, 5.0, 4.0, 5.0. This stream is used in every diagram below.

The middle moves as numbers arrive: 5, then between 5 and 15, then back to 5.

Solution 1 — Brute force: sort every time you are asked

addNum just tosses the number on the end of a list. findMedian sorts the whole list, then reads the middle. Simple, but every question pays for a sort.
the list (nums)

Speed & memory: addNum is O(1), but each findMedian sorts: O(n log n) in general, O(n) here because only the new value is out of place. A median after every add costs O(n²) in total; the notebook measured about 4× time per doubling. O(n) memory.

Solution 2 — Better: keep one list always sorted

Never let the list get out of order. Binary search finds where the new number belongs (bisect.insort), it is slotted in there, and the median is just "look at the middle index". The cost moved: every value to the right of the slot has to shift one place.
the sorted list (nums)

Speed & memory: finding the slot is O(log n), but the shift makes each addNum O(n); findMedian is O(1). On a decreasing stream (every number goes to the front) the notebook measured roughly 3 to 4.5× per doubling: O(n²) in total. O(n) memory.

Solution 3 — The smart way: two heaps that meet in the middle

We never need the whole list in order, only the values next to the middle. So split the numbers into two piles. low holds the smaller half and always shows its biggest on top (a max-heap). high holds the bigger half and shows its smallest on top (a min-heap). A heap is a pile that hands you its top in one step and takes a new value in O(log n).
Every addNum does the same three moves: 1. push the number into low; 2. move low's biggest into high (so nothing in low is bigger than anything in high); 3. if high now has more values, move its smallest back. Then low has the same count as high, or one more, and the median is low's top or the average of both tops.
low half (max-heap)  |  high half (min-heap), drawn in order so you can see the split

what Python actually stores (heapq lists; low keeps negatives because heapq is a min-heap)

Speed & memory: at most three heap pushes/pops per addNum: O(log n); findMedian just reads the tops: O(1). The notebook measured about 2× per doubling for n adds (O(n log n) in total). O(n) memory.

When you'd use this

See "median of a stream", "running middle value", or anything that needs the boundary between a lower and an upper group while values keep arriving? Keep a max-heap for the low side and a min-heap for the high side, balanced in size.
