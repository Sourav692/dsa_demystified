1046 · Last Stone Weight

Keep smashing the two heaviest stones together. What is left at the end?

The problem, in plain words

You have a pile of stones. Each turn, pick up the two heaviest and smash them. Equal weights: both turn to dust. Different weights: the lighter one breaks, and the heavier one shrinks by the lighter one's weight. Stop when one stone (or none) is left, and report its weight (0 if none).

Example: [2,7,4,1,8,1] → 1. The tricky part: the shrunken stone goes back into the pile, so "which two are heaviest?" must be answered again every turn.
being smashednew leftover stonelast stone (answer)

Bar height = stone weight. Each turn removes two bars and may add one shorter bar.

Solution 1 — Brute force: sort the whole pile every turn

Before each smash, sort the pile from light to heavy. The two heaviest are now the last two: pop them, smash, and drop the leftover at the end of the list. The leftover is out of place, so the next turn sorts again.

Speed & memory: up to n - 1 turns, each with a full sort: O(n² log n) as written. Python's sort notices the list is already sorted except the last stone, so in practice it grows like O(n²) (about 4× slower each time the pile doubles). O(n) memory for the copy.

Solution 2 — The smart way: a max-heap does the "find the heaviest" for you

A heap is a list kept in a loose order where the biggest item is always at the front (position 0). Taking it out or putting a new one in costs only about log n moves, not a full sort. Python's heapq keeps the smallest at the front, so we store weights as negative numbers; the boxes below show the real weights.

Speed & memory: build the heap once in O(n), then each turn is two pops and at most one push, O(log n) each: O(n log n) total, about 2× per doubling. O(n) memory.

When you'd use this

See "each turn, take the largest (or smallest)", "combine the two biggest", or "repeat until one is left"? Simulate it, and keep the pile in a heap so every turn is O(log n).
