Build a tiny Twitter: post, follow, unfollow, and show the 10 newest tweets in your feed.

The problem, in plain words

Every user can post tweets and follow other users. Your feed is the 10 most recent tweets written by you or by anyone you follow, newest first. Following yourself, or unfollowing yourself, changes nothing: you always see your own tweets.

Each tweet gets a time stamp from one shared clock (0, 1, 2, …). A bigger time means a newer tweet. Each user's own tweets are stored in the order they were posted, so every user's list is already sorted by time. That is the whole trick.

Tweets are shown as id with their time stamp t. Posting only appends to the end of one user's list.

Solution 1 — Brute force: gather everything, sort, keep 10

To build user 1's feed, copy user 1's tweets and every followee's tweets into one pile, sort the pile newest first, and return the first 10 ids. Simple, but it sorts every tweet even though only 10 are needed.

Speed & memory: post / follow / unfollow are instant, O(1). Each feed copies and sorts all T tweets of you and your followees — O(T log T) time, O(T) memory. In the notebook, doubling the tweets roughly doubles the feed time.

Solution 2 — The smart way: merge the k timelines with a heap

Treat each user (you plus each followee) as one sorted list and read it from the newest end. Put each list's newest tweet into a heap that always hands back the most recent one. Pop it into the feed, then push the next-older tweet from the same user. Stop after 10 pops. This is Merge k Sorted Lists, run backwards in time.

Speed & memory: with k = followees + 1, a feed costs O(k + 10 log k) time and O(k) memory, no matter how many tweets exist. In the notebook its time stays flat (about 1x) while the tweets double.

When you'd use this

See "the N most recent across several feeds", "news feed / timeline" or "merge k sorted streams"? Keep each source sorted as it grows, then merge them with a heap and stop as soon as you have N items.
