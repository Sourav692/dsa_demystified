4 · Median of Two Sorted Arrays

Two sorted lists. Find the middle value of all of them together, without gluing them together.

The problem, in plain words

Two teams each lined up by height, shortest first. Who would stand in the middle if both teams merged into one line? That person's height is the median (the middle value). With an even number of people there are two middle people, and the median is the average of their two heights.

Example: [1, 3] and [2] → merged [1, 2, 3] → 2.0. And [1, 2] and [3, 4] → (2 + 3) / 2 = 2.5.
The catch: the answer must take O(log (m + n)) time. Just reading every value is already too slow, so the fast solution never builds the merged line.

The walkthrough below uses nums1 = [2, 4, 7, 10, 14, 17], nums2 = [8, 11, 16, 19] (answer 10.5), chosen so the fast method has to move in both directions.

Solution 1 — Brute force: merge everything, then pick the middle

Put a finger on the front of each list. Copy the smaller of the two values into a new list and move that finger. When one list runs out, copy the rest of the other. Then read the middle of the new list.

Speed & memory: every value is copied once — O(m + n) time — and the new list holds them all — O(m + n) memory.

Solution 2 — Better: walk to the middle and stop

Same two fingers, but stop as soon as you reach the middle, and remember only the last two values you passed (prev and cur). No new list is built. With 10 values, the middle is merged positions 4 and 5, so 6 steps are enough.

remembered values

Speed & memory: about half the steps of Solution 1, but still O(m + n) time; only two values are kept — O(1) memory.

Solution 3 — The smart way: binary search the cut in the smaller list

The 5 smallest values (the left half) are made of some front part of one list plus some front part of the other. If the left half takes i values from the smaller list A, it must take j = 5 - i from B. So only i is unknown. Guess i in the middle, look at the four values touching the two cuts, and they tell you to move left, move right, or stop.

the four values touching the cuts (missing neighbours count as −∞ / +∞)

Speed & memory: each guess halves the possible cuts in the smaller list — O(log min(m, n)) time, O(1) memory. On a million values per list it takes about 20 guesses.

When you'd use this

See two sorted lists together with "median", "k-th smallest", or a required O(log (m + n))? Don't merge. Binary search how many values the left side takes from the smaller list; the other list's share is then fixed.
