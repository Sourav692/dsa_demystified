84 · Largest Rectangle in Histogram

The biggest rectangle that fits under a row of bars.

The problem, in plain words

Bars of width 1 stand side by side. Find the biggest rectangle you can draw inside the bars. A rectangle that spans several bars can only be as tall as the shortest of them.

Example: [2, 1, 5, 6, 2, 3] → 10: bars 2 and 3 (heights 5 and 6) at height 5, width 2.
bar we are looking aton the stack / scannedinside the rectanglea wall (shorter bar)

Every best rectangle is as tall as one of its bars. So: for each bar, stretch left and right until a shorter bar stops you.

Solution 1 — Brute force: every left edge, every right edge

Pick a left bar, then slide the right edge one bar at a time, keeping the shortest height seen so far. Each step gives one rectangle: shortest height × width. Keep the biggest.

Speed & memory: about n²/2 (left, right) pairs — O(n²) time, O(1) extra memory.

Solution 2 — Two passes: find each bar's walls

A bar's own rectangle stretches until the nearest shorter bar on each side. Find the left wall for every bar in one left-to-right pass with a stack (pop bars that are not shorter; what is left on top is the wall), then the right wall in a right-to-left pass. Area = height × (right - left - 1).

Solution 3 — The smart way: one pass, compute the width when a bar is popped

Keep bar indices on a stack whose heights only go up. When a shorter bar arrives at i, every taller bar on top can't grow any further right: pop it. Its right wall is i, and its left wall is the bar now below it on the stack. Area = height × (i - left - 1). A height-0 bar after the end pops whatever is left.

When you'd use this

See "largest rectangle", "maximum area under a skyline" or "the span where this element is the smallest"? For each element you need the nearest smaller element on both sides: an increasing stack gives both in one pass.
