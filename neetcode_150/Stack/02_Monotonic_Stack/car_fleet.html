853 · Car Fleet

Cars on a one-lane road can't overtake. How many groups reach the finish?

The problem, in plain words

Cars drive one way down a one-lane road to a finish line. Nobody can overtake. A fast car that catches a slow one has to slow down and they drive on together as a fleet. How many fleets cross the finish line?

Example: finish at 12, positions [10, 8, 0, 5, 3], speeds [2, 4, 1, 1, 3] → 3 fleets.
One number per car tells you everything: its solo arrival time (12 - position) / speed. A car catches the one ahead if it would arrive no later than it.

Labels under each car: position, speed, and solo arrival time t.

Solution 1 — Brute force: check every car ahead

For each car, look at every car ahead of it. If any of them would arrive no earlier on its own, our car is stuck behind it (or behind the fleet it is in). Otherwise our car is the front of its own fleet: count it.

fleets counted

Speed & memory: every car checks every other car — O(n²) time; one arrival time per car — O(n) memory.

Solution 2 — The smart way: sort by position, stack the fleet times

Line the cars up from the finish line backwards. Keep a stack of fleet arrival times; the top is the fleet just ahead. A car that would arrive no later than the top catches that fleet and disappears into it. A car that would arrive later starts a new, slower fleet: push its time. The times on the stack only ever go up.

stack of fleet arrival times (top on the right)

Speed & memory: sorting costs O(n log n), the sweep is O(n); the sorted list and stack use O(n) memory.

When you'd use this

See "cannot pass", "catches up and moves together" or "how many groups arrive"? Sort by position, turn each item into one number (its arrival time), and sweep from the front keeping a stack of group times.
