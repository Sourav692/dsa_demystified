739 · Daily Temperatures

For every day: how long until it gets warmer?

The problem, in plain words

You have a list of daily temperatures. For each day, count how many days you must wait until a warmer day comes. If no warmer day ever comes, write 0.

Example: [73, 74, 75, 71, 69, 72, 76, 73] → [1, 1, 4, 2, 1, 1, 0, 0]. Day 2 (75°) waits 4 days, until 76° on day 6.

The answer for a day is a distance: the index of its warmer day minus its own index.

Solution 1 — Brute force: look right from every day

For each day, walk to the right one day at a time until you find a warmer one. Simple, but a day with no warmer day walks all the way to the end.

answer

Speed & memory: each day may scan everything after it, so it gets slow fast — O(n²) time, O(1) extra memory.

Solution 2 — Right to left, jumping along known answers

Fill the answers from the last day backwards. If the next day is not warmer, you don't need to check the days it was waiting through: jump straight to its warmer day, which you already know. If it has none, neither do you.

answer (filled right to left)

Solution 3 — The smart way: a stack of days still waiting

Walk left to right. Keep a stack of the days that have not found a warmer day yet; their temperatures only go down from bottom to top. Each new day pops every cooler day from the top — it is their warmer day — and writes the distance. Then it waits on the stack itself.

answer

Speed & memory: every day is pushed once and popped at most once — O(n) time; the stack can hold every day — O(n) memory.

When you'd use this

See "how many days until", "next warmer / greater / taller element" or "the first element to the right that beats this one"? Keep the elements still waiting on a stack and let each new element pop the ones it beats.
